\section{Conclusion}
Collective forecasts on temporal operational graphs require more than accurate
individual event probabilities. In these traces, shared dependence, marginal
bias and typed incidence contribute differently across applications and
projections. Strong direct count models can outperform more elaborate
entity-scenario constructions, while group-risk scores reveal behavior that
count CRPS alone does not capture. The practical contribution is a reproducible
evaluation contract and controlled benchmark: compare shared and typed
dependence, test recent same-version calibration, include direct and reconciled
count alternatives, and report the intended operational quantity separately.
