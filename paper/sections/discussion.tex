\section{Discussion and limitations}
\paragraph{What a collective forecast adds.}
The factor sampler provides a distribution over entity outcomes that can be
projected onto several overlapping groups without refitting a separate count
model. This is useful when the same task belongs to a job and a machine, or
when an analyst changes the grouping after generation. The historical-residual
comparator offers a different advantage: it can adjust aggregate bias directly.
Its projections do not, in general, arise from a common set of entity outcomes.
Neither property alone guarantees better predictive scores. The experiments
compare these practical tradeoffs under a declared target and operating regime.

\paragraph{Marginal bias is not fixed by a copula.}
Every matched-marginal dependence control has the same expected group count,
$\mathbb E[C_{Ak}]=\sum_{i\in A}F_{ik}$. Changing shared factors can change
dispersion and tails, but cannot repair a systematically biased mean. Apparent
improvements in a distributional score may therefore coexist with a worse
decision quantile. Moreover, composite-likelihood fitting can attribute common
forecast errors to dependence. A learned global or relation factor is a
predictive construction, not proof of a physical common cause.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
The two logs are single organizational settings, and the final cluster period
contains only seven days. Millions of entity queries do not create millions of
independent temporal replications. The block intervals describe variation
within these finite traces; they cannot establish universal superiority or
reliability in a new cluster or bank. The relatively short development periods
also constrain tuning and historical residual estimation. Architecture, width,
history length, and pair sampling are practical choices rather than exhaustive
hyperparameter optima. Published point-process and full simulation systems
remain important unimplemented numerical comparisons.

The factor families model positive association and use memberships observed at
the origin. They do not explicitly simulate future membership changes,
arrivals, repeated transitions, resource allocation, or counterfactual policies.
Dependence across horizons is restricted by one latent event time per entity.
First exits can be operationally useful while still being an incomplete
description of total future workload. BPI last-handler groups include
administrative/system behavior and do not identify exclusive worker occupancy.
The normalized reserve loss is a transparent forecast-based proxy, not measured
economic savings or a staffing recommendation.

\paragraph{Implications for scientific dataset generation.}
The reusable artifact is a timestamp-bounded adapter, an explicit outcome
contract, and a collection of scenario diagnostics. A future dataset-generation
system should preserve these contracts while adding arrivals and subsequent
state transitions. Generating a visually plausible event stream is insufficient
evidence of faithful operational uncertainty. Such a system would also need
downstream utility, privacy, and policy-validity experiments; none are claimed
by this study. The present data are public research releases, and original
license terms and attribution remain attached to their sources.
