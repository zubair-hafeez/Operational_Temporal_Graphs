\begin{abstract}
Operational forecasts often concern groups of related entities, while
individual event probabilities leave their collective uncertainty unspecified.
We construct a temporal-graph forecasting benchmark for the joint first exits
of the population active at a forecast origin, using a compute-cluster trace
and a loan-application workflow. The benchmark separates individual-model
selection from dependence modeling: tree and temporal neural predictors supply
fixed marginal CDFs, while classical global, typed relation, and
context-dependent factors generate coherent multihorizon entity outcomes.
Controls include two dependence families, risk-stratified incidence
permutation, and historical aggregate-error resampling. A frozen future-period
evaluation covers 28 cluster origins and 61 workflow origins. The selected
model reduces normalized count CRPS by 24.3\% in the cluster and 8.3\% in the
workflow relative to independent sampling. Yet a declared reserve-loss proxy
improves by 59.7\% in the cluster and worsens by 9.6\% in the workflow.
Observed relation incidence contributes beyond individual risk, while richer
context networks do not establish a consistent advantage. Simple aggregate
residual resampling is the strongest workflow CRPS comparator. These findings
support evaluating marginal bias, joint distributions, and decision loss
separately. The contribution is a reproducible application benchmark and
scalable reference implementation, with explicit limits on calibration,
temporal evidence, and operational interpretation.
\end{abstract}
