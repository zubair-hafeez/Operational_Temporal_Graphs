# Evidence-driven scope revision

The original exploration proposed broad behavioral event-log generation and downstream synthetic-data utility. The implemented and tested task is more precise: **joint, multihorizon first-exit forecasts for the entities active at a forecast origin**, conditioned on their observed temporal graph. This remains an application of generative learning, but it does not generate future arrivals, exact continuous timestamps, all event marks, or arbitrary future graph topology. No result from this project should be described as a successful full event-log simulator or synthetic training-data factory.

The practical question is broad across operational systems: when many related entities are currently active, how uncertain are their future collective outcomes? The instantiated outcomes are changes of pending/running task state in a compute cluster and application resolution in a bank workflow. The common object is the distribution of current-cohort survivors and exits across several horizons, with observed job, machine, or last-handler relations. New arrivals and return transitions require additional models. A future dataset factory can reuse the timestamp-bounded adapters, outcome contracts, and scenario diagnostics.

This revision follows three empirical findings. First, graph summaries do not consistently improve individual forecasts over personal history. Second, simple shared factors often match or beat context-dependent neural dependence. Third, distributional score improvements need not improve an asymmetric operational cost proxy, especially under marginal bias. A credible paper must expose these distinctions rather than claiming a universal benefit from graph complexity.

The proposed contribution is a reproducible operational forecasting study and a scalable implementation for changing entity populations. The Gaussian-factor and common-shock distributions are classical. Composite likelihood, copulas, proper scores, and temporal mixing encoders are established methods. Do not claim those ingredients, correlation within jobs, or the general importance of dependence as discoveries. The substantive evidence must concern the constructed applications, comparison with strong temporal predictors, matched-marginal controls, observed-incidence interventions, and out-of-time operational performance.

Primary experiments compare independent draws, global factors, static typed relation factors, and context-dependent factors with exactly the same individual CDFs. A risk-stratified permutation changes only which observed entities share relation noise. This is a model intervention, not an intervention on the real system and not causal identification. A separate benchmark compares HGB feature/calibration variants and three-seed Mixer, GRU, and MLP ensembles. The temporal Mixer is an explicit first-exit adaptation, not an exact reproduction of GraphMixer's original link-prediction benchmark.

The reserve-cost experiment is a transparent proxy: choose a quantile of the number of unexited members of the current cohort, with under/over penalties 3 or 9. It measures neither currency nor realized savings after deploying a policy. BPI last-handler membership is not exclusive worker occupancy. Borg resource requests are not measured utilization. A scope-limited proxy is preferable to unsupported deployment claims, but this limitation must remain prominent in the manuscript.

The original PROTOCOL.md is retained as the exploration record. Final evaluation will follow a separately frozen manifest containing this revised task, selected marginal models, all comparison modes, horizons, forecast origins, bootstrap blocks, and artifact hashes. The final periods remain unopened until that freeze. This is a development-informed freeze, not a claim of prospective preregistration.

The last literature check identified an especially close prior work: Avramenko, Chapela-Campa, Dumas and Milani, *Short-term business process simulation: current-state initialization and uncertainty quantification*, Process Science 3:12 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1007/s44311-026-00045-9 . It reconstructs ongoing process states and learns workload-conditional error intervals, including experiments on BPI 2017. Current-state operational uncertainty is therefore not a novel problem in itself. The manuscript must distinguish the matched-marginal joint-dependence experiment and cross-application projection benchmark, and must acknowledge that it does not numerically reproduce that complete simulation system.

A historical aggregate residual comparator was added before final access. It stores standardized multihorizon development count errors, resamples one origin and size-matched group error vectors, and generates clipped/rounded/monotone direct count forecasts. Its development assessment leaves out each scored origin; its final assessment uses the frozen development error bank. It can repair group means and is not a matched-marginal or entity-coherent comparator. This baseline is strong on BPI development; excluding it would misrepresent the difficulty of the application.
