Implemented a classical shared-factor Gaussian-copula scenario baseline with identical calibrated marginals across independent/correlated samplers. Selected factor weights on development count CRPS. Borg pending selected global variance 0.2 + job variance 0.5; sampled-population count-fraction CRPS 0.072532 independent versus 0.047820 coupled. Independent 90% aggregate intervals cover very poorly in these pilots. This could partly reflect marginal misspecification/common shifts; it is not proof that the fitted relations cause outcomes. Full-cell assessment, stronger marginals, forward cross-fitting, state-conditioned classical controls, and block uncertainty remain necessary.

Prior-art follow-up: TACTiS/TACTiS-2, spatial Gaussian copulas trained with scoring rules, graph-copula time-series models, and classical frailty establish that graph/correlated sampling alone is not a novel contribution. Any paper must demonstrate an operationally useful, scalable approach for changing active-entity populations and explicitly compare shared-factor controls. BPI may serve as a negative/weak-relation case; do not conceal that result or force a graph gain.

## 2026-10-14 Disjoint-fit coupling pilots and larger development data

Implemented Gaussian factor dependence with differentiable bivariate normal probabilities and three matched modes: global factor (2 parameters), static global/relation/private factors (3 parameters), and context-dependent factors (2,211 Borg / 4,387 BPI parameters). Node marginals are held fixed. Trained on separate outcomes from the marginal predictor/calibrator; checkpoint selection used validation pair likelihood. GPU0/Borg and GPU1/BPI seed-0 pilots completed, preserving other jobs. The first sandbox attempt could not access CUDA; its empty failed directory/log are retained and host training succeeded. The disjoint-fit pilot uses much less marginal training data than the earlier pilot and is not directly comparable to it.

Scenario-score results remain mixed: context adaptation does not consistently improve over static/global coupling. Added classical exponential common-shock (Marshall–Olkin family) controls with exact marginal preservation and pair probabilities; results also do not support a sweeping architecture claim. Strong simple dependence baselines are mandatory. The original Marshall–Olkin reference is [JASA 1967, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1967.10482885](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1967.10482885). The common-shock model here is associational, not an identified cause of operational failures.
